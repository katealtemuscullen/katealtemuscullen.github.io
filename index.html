My research examines the governance and political economy of climate change adaptation, with a focus on water. I take a mixed methods and participatory research approach, integrating field-based qualitative analysis, household surveying, and computational decision-support modeling.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar at the Stanford Doerr School of Sustainability, collaborating with Sarah Fletcher in the Department of Civil & Environmental Engineering. I received my PhD in environmental social sciences from the Energy & Resources Group at the University of California, Berkeley.

My work has been supported by NASA ROSES, NSF, Fulbright-Hays, and Fulbright, among others.

Research Portfolio

My research lies at the intersection of political economy, institutional governance, and climate adaptation. I study how political economic systems, institutions, and planning processes shape adaptation and adaptive capacity and deepen or disrupt inequalities in resource access. My approach is interdisciplinary, multi-scalar, and participatory. I bridge qualitative and quantitative field-based methods in surveying and interviewing with computational systems modeling. My research portfolio reflects the breadth of my empirical interests, from drought adaptation and water management in the Americas to the evolution of global climate governance. My ongoing and recent projects include:

  1. Rethinking decision-support models for politically feasible climate adaptation plans (2026–Present)
  2. Glacier retreat and water reliability in Santiago, Chile (2024–2026)
  3. Drinking water access and adaptive capacity to megadrought in rural Chile (2023–2026)
  4. The institutional architecture and evolution of global climate governance (2021–Present)
  5. Water markets as climate adaptation: pitfalls and tradeoffs (2020–2021)
